The six tones

Every syllable carries a tone, marked on the vowel. The tone is part of the word: say it wrong and you may say a different word. "Sounds like" spellings are rough English approximations of Hanoi pronunciation.

ToneMarkHow to say itExample
Flat (ngang)none: aLevel and mid-high, like holding a notema (ghost)
Falling (huyền)àLow and sliding down, like a sighmà (but)
Rising (sắc)áSharp rise, like "what?"má (cheek)
Dipping (hỏi)ảDrops, then comes back upmả (tomb)
Broken (ngã)ãRises with a catch in the throat midwaymã (code)
Heavy (nặng)ạLow, short, cut off abruptlymạ (rice seedling)

You and I

Tôi (I) and bạn (you) are neutral and safe for a visitor. Locals usually swap in age-based words such as anh (older man), chị (older woman) and em (younger person).
