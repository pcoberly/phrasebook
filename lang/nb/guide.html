Norwegian

This is Bokmål, the written Norwegian most Norwegians use. Norwegians speak many dialects, but everyone understands this standard, and Swedes and Danes follow it well. Nearly every Norwegian speaks excellent English, so these phrases are about the smile. The "sounds like" spellings put the stressed syllable in capitals; Norwegian has a gentle rise and fall, so copy the audio.

Letters to know

LettersSound like
åThe o in "more": går (gohr)
æThe ai in "air"
øThe u in "fur" with rounded lips, written ö: øl (öl), beer
u, yoo and ee said with rounded lips, written ü: du (dü), you
sk before i, y, ø; skj, sjsh: sju (shü), seven
kjA soft sh: kjøpe (SHÖ-peh), to buy
hvv, the h is silent: hva (vaa), what
j, gjy: jeg (yai), I

Being polite

Norwegians are informal: du for everyone, first names everywhere. Takk is said constantly, including takk for maten, thanks for the food, after a meal. Norway is expensive: tap water is excellent and free, and outdoor life, friluftsliv, is a way of life.
