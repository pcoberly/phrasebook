Czech

Czech is spoken by about 10 million people, and Slovaks understand it easily. It is spelled very regularly, and the stress is always on the first syllable, shown in capitals in the "sounds like" spellings. A mark over a vowel (á, é, í, ó, ú, ů, ý) makes it long, not stressed.

Words without vowels

Czech can build words around r or l alone: vlk is a wolf, Brno is said BR-no, and there is even a famous sentence with no vowels at all, Strč prst skrz krk, stick a finger through your throat.

Being polite

Greet with dobrý den when you enter a shop and na shledanou when you leave. Use the polite forms ending in -te with strangers, as these phrases do. When toasting, look each person in the eye.
