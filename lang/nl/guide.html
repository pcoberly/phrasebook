Which Dutch

This is Dutch as spoken in the Netherlands, in the polite u form. Flemish Dutch in Belgium is very close, with a softer g and some different words. The "sounds like" spellings put the stressed syllable in capitals. Nearly everyone in the Netherlands also speaks English, so a few Dutch words are a courtesy rather than a necessity.

The g and ch

Dutch g and ch are made at the back of the throat, like the ch in "loch" or a clearing of the throat. The spellings here use "kh" for it. In Belgium the g is softer.

You, polite and casual

U is the polite you, and it is right for shop staff, waiters and older people. Je (or jij) is the casual you, and many Dutch people use it with strangers in shops and cafés without meaning anything by it. The phrases here use u, which is never wrong.

De and het

Dutch has two words for "the": de (most nouns) and het (neuter nouns, and all small things ending in -je). You mostly learn them with each noun: de rekening, het toilet, het ontbijt. Using the wrong one is a small mistake that is easily forgiven.

Numbers and money

One to ten first. From 21 the units come before the tens: eenentwintig is "one and twenty". Prices are in euros, and cash is less common than a debit card (pinnen).
