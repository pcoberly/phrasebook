Hungarian

Hungarian, Magyar, is spoken by about 13 million people. It isn't related to its neighbors' languages: its closest relatives are Finnish and Estonian, distantly. The good news: spelling is regular, the stress is always on the first syllable, shown in capitals in the "sounds like" spellings, and there is no he or she, so every phrase is the same for a man or a woman.

Endings instead of words

Hungarian adds endings where English uses small words: Debrecen, but Debrecenbe, to Debrecen; mogyoró, peanut, but mogyoróra, to peanuts. The vowel in the ending matches the word's own vowels.

Being polite

These phrases use the polite form, right for strangers. Hungarians put the family name first: Bartók Béla, not Béla Bartók. Many don't clink beer glasses, an old custom.
