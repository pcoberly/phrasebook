Spelled as it sounds

Indonesian uses the Latin alphabet and is spelled almost exactly as it is said, so once you know a few letters you can read any sign. There are no tones and no genders. Stress is light, usually on the second-to-last syllable, and the voice rises at the end of a question. "Sounds like" spellings are rough English approximations.

Being polite

Give and take things, and shake hands, with your right hand; pointing with the thumb is politer than with a finger. Ending a request with ya softens it, and tolong, minta and boleh make it polite. Don't touch anyone's head.

Numbers and money

Numbers are regular: belas makes the teens (dua belas, 12), puluh the tens (dua puluh, 20), ratus hundreds, ribu thousands and juta millions. Se- means one: seratus, seribu. Prices are in rupiah and run into the thousands, written with dots (Rp 25.000) and often shortened to 25 rb, or just said as dua puluh lima.
