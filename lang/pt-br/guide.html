Which Portuguese

This is Brazilian Portuguese, as spoken in Rio and São Paulo. Portugal's Portuguese is written much the same but sounds very different, and some everyday words differ. The "sounds like" spellings put the stressed syllable in capitals.

Nasal vowels

A vowel with a tilde (ã, õ), or before m or n at the end of a syllable, is said partly through the nose, as in French: não (nown), bem (bayn), sim (seen). The spellings write it with n or ng, but don't fully say that n.

Man or woman

A few words change with the speaker's gender. A man says obrigado, thank you, and a woman says obrigada, whoever they are thanking. Where a phrase differs, the woman's form is shown under it as "A woman says", and the female voice says that form.

Being polite

Você is the everyday you, fine with almost anyone. To an older person or in a formal setting, o senhor (to a man) and a senhora (to a woman) show respect. Brazilians are warm and informal: a smile, por favor and obrigado go a long way. The thumbs up is used everywhere to mean thanks or OK, but the OK sign made with a circle of finger and thumb is rude.
