The alphabet

Russian is written in Cyrillic. It's worth an evening to learn: signs, menus and metro stations become readable, and many words turn out to be familiar (ресторан is restaurant, такси taxi). The "sounds like" spellings are rough English approximations, with the stressed syllable in capitals.

Stress

There are no tones, but every word has one stressed syllable, and it can fall anywhere. Russian writing doesn't mark it, so learn it with the word. Stress also changes the vowels: an unstressed о sounds like a (молоко, ma-la-KO), and an unstressed е or я sounds close to i (меня, mee-NYA).

Being polite

Use вы (vih), the polite you, with anyone you don't know; ты (tih) is for friends and children. Пожалуйста (please) does a lot of work: it also means you're welcome and here you are. A man and a woman say a few phrases differently, such as I would like: the card shows what a woman says, and the female voice says it. Russians don't smile at strangers much; it isn't unfriendly.

Numbers and money

Prices are in rubles (рубль, written ₽ or руб.). Thousands are separated by a space: 1 500 ₽. The word for ruble changes with the number: один рубль, два рубля, пять рублей. Cards issued outside Russia often don't work there, so carry cash.
