Bulgarian

Bulgarian is spoken by about 7 million people. It is written in Cyrillic, which was in fact created in Bulgaria in the 9th century, so street signs are worth learning to read. The "sounds like" spellings put the stressed syllable in capitals; stress can fall anywhere, so copy the audio.

Yes is a shake, no is a nod

Traditionally, Bulgarians shake the head for yes and nod for no, the opposite of most of the world. Many adapt for visitors, which makes it even more confusing. Say да (da) or не (ne) and listen for the word.

Man or woman

Some Bulgarian verbs and adjectives change with the speaker's gender, so these phrases use forms that are the same for anyone: Може ли...?, may I have...?, and Имам алергия към..., I have an allergy to.

Being polite

These phrases use the polite plural you, right for strangers. Bulgarians are hospitable: expect rakia and a shopska salad at the start of a meal, and look everyone in the eye when you toast.
