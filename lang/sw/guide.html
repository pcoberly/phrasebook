Which Swahili

Swahili, Kiswahili, is spoken across East Africa: it is a national language of Kenya and Tanzania and widely used in Uganda, Rwanda and eastern Congo. Most speakers learned it alongside a local language, so it's used to everyone hearing it with an accent, and people are delighted when visitors try. This is the standard Swahili of Tanzania and the Kenyan coast.

Easy to read

Swahili is written as it sounds, with no silent letters. The five vowels are pure, as in Spanish or Italian: a as in "father", e as in "bed", i as in "feet", o as in "more", u as in "food". Every vowel is said, even side by side: naomba is na-om-ba. The "sounds like" spellings put the stressed syllable in capitals, and it is almost always the second to last.

Being polite

Greetings matter: always greet before asking anything, and take time over it. Ask about someone's news, Habari?, and they'll ask about yours. Greet older people with Shikamoo, "I hold your feet", a sign of respect; they answer Marahaba. Use your right hand to give, take and eat. Polepole, slowly, gently, is good advice too.

Swahili time

Swahili counts the hours from dawn, not midnight. Saa moja, the first hour, is 7 am, and saa nne, the fourth hour, is 10 am. When someone gives you a time, check which kind they mean.

Words you already know

Safari just means journey, hakuna matata means no worries, and simba is lion. Swahili also borrows from Arabic, Portuguese and English: meza, table, comes from Portuguese, and basi, teksi and treni need no explaining.
