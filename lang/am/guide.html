Amharic

Amharic is Ethiopia's working language, spoken across the country alongside Oromo, Tigrinya, and dozens of others. It is a Semitic language, a cousin of Arabic and Hebrew, written in its own Ge'ez script (ፊደል, fidel), from left to right. Each symbol is a syllable: the base shape is the consonant, and small changes to it add the vowel, so ለ is le, ሉ lu, ሊ li, ላ la. The "sounds like" spellings put the stressed syllable in capitals.

Polite forms

Amharic changes "you" for a man, a woman, and politeness. These phrases use the polite forms (እርስዎ, ir-SI-wo), which are the same whoever you speak to and right for anyone older or unknown. The notes give the casual forms friends use with each other.

Ethiopian time and calendar

Ethiopia counts the hours from 6 in the morning, so 7 am is "1 o'clock" and noon is "6 o'clock". It also has its own calendar of 13 months, about seven and a half years behind the Western one. When someone gives a time or a date, ask whether it is Ethiopian or "ferenji" (foreign).

Being polite

Greetings are long and warm: shake hands, often with the left hand touching your right forearm, and ask after the person, their family, their health. Eat with your right hand, from the shared plate of injera in front of you. Being offered food from someone's hand, gursha, is a sign of affection: accept it.
