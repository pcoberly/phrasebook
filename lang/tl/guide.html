Tagalog and Filipino

Tagalog is the language of Manila and the base of Filipino, the national language, and is understood across the Philippines. It uses the Latin alphabet and is spelled much as it sounds. Many words come from Spanish (mesa, kape, asukal), and mixing in English, called Taglish, is completely normal. "Sounds like" spellings are rough English approximations.

Stress matters

There are no tones, but stress can change the meaning: BU-kas is open, bu-KAS is tomorrow. The spelling usually doesn't show it, so listen to the audio; the capitals in the "sounds like" spellings mark the stressed syllable. Some words, like hindi (no), end with a catch in the throat, as in the middle of "uh-oh".

Po and opo

Po makes almost any sentence polite, and opo is the polite yes. Use them with anyone older than you, with strangers and in shops; they are dropped with friends. Kayo is the polite you, ikaw the familiar one.

Numbers and money

Tagalog numbers are used for counting, but for prices and the time people usually switch to English or to Spanish numbers (singkwenta, 50; alas dos, two o'clock). Prices are in pesos (₱), written with commas as in English: ₱1,500.
