Ukrainian

Ukrainian is the state language of Ukraine and the language most Ukrainians choose to speak. It is written in Cyrillic, like Russian, but it is a separate language with its own letters, words and sounds, and greeting people in Ukrainian is warmly appreciated. The "sounds like" spellings put the stressed syllable in capitals. Stress can fall on any syllable and changes meaning, so copy the audio.

Letters to know

їyee: їхати (YEE-kha-tih), to travel
єye, as in "yes": є (ye), there is
гA soft, voiced h, never g: гарно (HAR-no), beautiful
ґA hard g, as in "go" (rare)
хkh, a rasp like the ch in "loch": хочу (KHO-choo)
вv, or w at the end of a syllable: Львів (lveew)
щshch, as in "fresh cheese": що (shcho), what
ж ч ш цzh, ch, sh and ts
ь, 'The soft sign softens the consonant before it; the apostrophe keeps a consonant hard before я, ю, є, ї: п'ять (pyat)

Man or woman

Past-tense verbs change with the speaker's gender. A man says Я б хотів (kho-TEEV), I'd like, and a woman says Я б хотіла (kho-TEE-la). Where a phrase differs, the woman's form is shown under it as "A woman says", and the female voice says that form. Most phrases here are the same for everyone.

Endings

Ukrainian nouns change their endings with their role in the sentence. After до (do), to, a place changes: Львів becomes до Львова, Київ (KIH-yeev), Kyiv, becomes до Києва (KIH-ye-va). If you get it wrong, people will still understand.

Being polite

Use ви (vih), the respectful you, with anyone you don't know, and будь ласка often. Please be thoughtful: Ukraine is at war, and many people are dealing with loss and hardship.
