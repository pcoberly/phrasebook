The script

Thai has its own alphabet, written left to right with no spaces between words, only between phrases. Vowels can sit before, after, above or below a consonant. Romanised spellings on signs and menus vary a lot, so trust the audio. The "sounds like" spellings here are rough English approximations.

The five tones

Every syllable has a tone, and it is part of the word: mǎa is dog, máa is horse. The "sounds like" spellings mark the tones with accents.

Khráp and khâ

Polite sentences end with a particle that depends on who is speaking: men say ครับ (kráp), women say ค่ะ (kâ), or คะ (ká, high tone) in questions. The word for I changes too: men say ผม (pǒm), women ฉัน (chǎn). Each card shows what a woman says, and the female voice says it. On its own, khráp or khâ also means yes.

Being polite

Greet with a wai: palms together at your chest and a slight bow; the higher the hands, the more respect. Keep your voice calm, smile, and don't touch anyone's head or point your feet at people or at images of the Buddha. Show respect for the royal family.

Numbers and money

Thai has its own digits (๑๒๓), but Arabic numerals are used almost everywhere. Prices are in baht. Big numbers have their own words: พัน (pan) is a thousand, หมื่น (mèun) ten thousand, แสน (sǎen) a hundred thousand and ล้าน (láan) a million.
