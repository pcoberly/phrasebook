Which Chinese

This is Mandarin (普通话, pǔtōnghuà), the standard spoken across mainland China, written in simplified characters. Under each phrase the app shows pinyin, the Latin spelling with tone marks, so you can say it before you can read the characters. Mandarin is also spoken in Taiwan and Singapore, with some different words and, in Taiwan, traditional characters.

The four tones

Mandarin has four tones, plus a light neutral one. The tone is part of the word, so say it wrong and you may say a different word: mā (mother) and mǎ (horse). The tone mark sits over the vowel in pinyin.

ToneMarkHow to say itExample
1st, high flatāHigh and level, like holding a notemā (mother)
2nd, risingáRises, like "what?"má (hemp)
3rd, dippingǎDips low, then risesmǎ (horse)
4th, fallingàFalls sharply, like a firm "no!"mà (to scold)
NeutralnoneShort and lightma (the question word)

Tone changes

WhenWhat happens

不 (bù) before a 4th toneIt becomes bú: bú shì
一 (yī) before a 4th toneIt becomes yí: yí biàn. Before the other tones it becomes yì: yìbǎi

Polite you, and counting words

你 (nǐ) is you, and 您 (nín) is the polite you, used with strangers and older people. When you count things Chinese uses a measure word between the number and the noun: 一张票 (a ticket), 一瓶啤酒 (a bottle of beer), 一辆车 (a car). 两 (liǎng) replaces 二 for two of something.

Money and paying

The currency is the yuan, spoken as 块 (kuài) and written ¥. Most shops, taxis and even street stalls take mobile payment (Alipay and WeChat Pay). Cash is legal tender, but not everywhere will take it, and foreign cards are accepted less than at home, so ask before you order.
