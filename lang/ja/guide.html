Three writing systems

Japanese mixes three scripts in one sentence. Under each phrase the app shows romaji (Japanese in Latin letters) so you can say it before you can read it.

ScriptUsed forExample

The five vowels

Japanese has only five vowels, and they are always pure, never gliding like English ones.

VowelSounds like
aah, as in "father"
iee, as in "feet" but shorter
uoo, with the lips relaxed, not rounded
eeh, as in "bed"
ooh, but short and pure

Politeness

Every phrase here uses the polite です (desu) and ます (masu) forms, which is right for shops, hotels and restaurants. Add -san to other people's names, but never to your own. Sumimasen is the all-purpose way to get a waiter's or shop assistant's attention.

Little words that matter

WordJob
ka (か)Ends a question. There is no question-mark sound, and no rising tone is needed
wa (は)Marks the topic. Written は but pronounced "wa"
o (を)Marks what you are asking for. Kore o kudasai = "this, please"
no (の)Links two things: namae no… = "name of…"

Numbers and prices

Four, seven and nine each have two readings. Use yon, nana and kyū, because shi (death) and ku (suffering) are best avoided. Prices are in yen, and large numbers count in tens of thousands: 10,000 is man (万), so 30,000 yen is san-man.
