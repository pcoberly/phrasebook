Polish

Polish is spoken by about 40 million people in Poland. It looks hard because of its consonant clusters, but it is spelled very regularly: once you know the letters, you can read anything. Stress almost always falls on the second to last syllable, shown in capitals in the "sounds like" spellings.

Man or woman

Polish verbs and adjectives change with the speaker's gender, so these phrases use forms that are the same for anyone: Poproszę..., I'll ask for, and Mam alergię na..., I have an allergy to.

Being polite

With strangers, Poles use pan (sir) and pani (madam) instead of "you": Czy mówi pan po angielsku?, literally "does the gentleman speak English?" Say dzień dobry when you walk into a shop. After do, to, place names change their ending: Kraków, but do Krakowa.
