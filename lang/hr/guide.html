Croatian, and its neighbors

This is Croatian, spoken by about 5 million people. Serbian, Bosnian, and Montenegrin are so close that people understand each other easily, so these phrases work across Croatia, Serbia, Bosnia and Herzegovina, and Montenegro. A few everyday words differ, and the notes give the Serbian and Bosnian ones. Serbian is often written in Cyrillic too. Croatian is spelled exactly as it sounds; the "sounds like" spellings put the stressed syllable in capitals.

Endings

Nouns change their endings with their role in the sentence: Split becomes do Splita, to Split, and water, voda, becomes vode in "do you have water?" People will understand you even if you keep the basic form.

Being polite

Use the polite vi forms, as these phrases do, with anyone you don't know. Coffee is a ritual: nobody rushes, and a cup on a terrace can last an hour. Toast with Živjeli! and look each person in the eye.
