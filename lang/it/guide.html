How Italian is spelled

Italian is spelled almost exactly as it sounds, so once you know these rules you can read nearly anything aloud. The "sounds like" spellings put the stressed syllable in capitals. Stress usually falls on the second-to-last syllable, and an accent mark such as è or città shows where it falls on the last.

The vowels

Five pure vowels, always clear and short, never gliding like English ones: a (ah), e (eh or ay), i (ee), o (oh or aw), u (oo). Every vowel in a word is pronounced, so Europa is "eh-oo-ROH-pah".

Double consonants

A double consonant is held for a beat longer than a single one, and it changes the word: pala (shovel) and palla (ball), caro (dear) and carro (cart). Say the pp in troppo and the tt in sette clearly.

You, polite and casual

Lei (the polite you, which takes the same verb ending as he or she) is right for shop staff, waiters and strangers: Parla inglese? Tu is for friends, family and young people: Parli inglese? The phrases here use Lei.

Masculine and feminine

Nouns are masculine or feminine and adjectives follow: il conto, la stazione, caro and cara. A speaker changes an ending to match their own gender: sono allergico (a man) and sono allergica (a woman).

Greetings through the day

Buongiorno until the early afternoon, then buonasera (good evening) from about three or four o'clock. Salve is a neutral hello at any hour, and ciao is casual, for friends. Greeting shop staff when you walk in, and saying arrivederci as you leave, is expected.
