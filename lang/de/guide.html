Which German

This is standard German as spoken in Germany, in the polite Sie form, which suits shop staff, waiters and strangers. The "sounds like" spellings put the stressed syllable in capitals. German is spelled much as it sounds, so once you know these rules you can read almost anything aloud.

You, polite and casual

Sie (always written with a capital S) is the polite you, and is right for anyone you don't know. Du is for friends, family and children. The phrases here use Sie, which takes the same verb ending as "they": Sprechen Sie Englisch?

Nouns and capitals

Every noun is written with a capital letter, and is masculine, feminine or neuter: der Bahnhof, die Rechnung, das Frühstück. The word for "the" changes with the gender, and you will mostly learn them with each noun. Long words are often several short words glued together: Busbahnhof is bus plus rail plus yard.

Numbers and money

Eins to zehn first. From 21 the units come before the tens: einundzwanzig is "one and twenty". Prices are in euros. Many small places take only cash.
