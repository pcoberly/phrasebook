Which Hebrew

This is modern Israeli Hebrew. It is written from right to left, and everyday writing leaves out the vowels, so the "sounds like" spellings matter more than usual. They put the stressed syllable in capitals.

Vowels

Hebrew has five short, pure vowels: a as in "father", e as in "bed", i as in "feet", o as in "more" and u as in "food". Small dots and dashes (niqqud) can mark them, but you will mostly see them only in children's books, prayer books and some signs.

Stress

Most words are stressed on the last syllable: to-DA, sha-LOM. Many everyday words are exceptions and stress the one before: BO-ker, MA-yim, ra-KE-vet.

Man or woman

Hebrew verbs and adjectives change with the speaker's gender. A man says אני לא מבין (a-NI lo me-VIN) and a woman says אני לא מבינה (a-NI lo me-vi-NA). Where a phrase differs, the woman's form is shown under it as "A woman says", and the female voice says that form. Some words are spelled the same and only sound different, such as רוצה: ro-TSE (a man) and ro-TSA (a woman).

"You" changes with the gender of the person you speak to, too. To keep things simple, the questions here use forms that are the same for anyone, mostly אפשר (ef-SHAR), meaning "is it possible", followed by a verb.

Small words that join on

Some short words are written joined to the next word: ה (ha) means the, ל (le) means to, ב (be) means in, and ו (ve) means and. So לחיפה (le-KHAI-fa) is "to Haifa".
