Finnish

Finnish is spoken by about 5 million people in Finland. It isn't related to Swedish or Russian: its relatives are Estonian and, distantly, Hungarian. The good news: it is spelled exactly as it sounds, the stress is always on the first syllable, shown in capitals in the "sounds like" spellings, and there is no he or she, so every phrase is the same for a man or a woman.

Long and short

A double letter is held twice as long, and it changes the meaning: tuli is fire, tuuli wind, tulli customs. Hold the double vowels in kiitos (KEE-tos) and Hyvää (HÜ-vää), and pause slightly on double consonants.

Letters to know

jy: juna (YU-na), train

Endings instead of words

Finnish adds endings where English uses small words: Turku, but Turkuun, to Turku; pähkinä, nut, but pähkinöille, to nuts.

Being polite

Finns value quiet and personal space, and mean what they say: small talk is short, and silence is comfortable. There's no everyday please; kiitos does the job. If you're offered a sauna, accept: it's the heart of Finnish life.
