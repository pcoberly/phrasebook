Which Persian

This is the Persian of Iran, Farsi, as spoken in Tehran. Afghans speak Dari and Tajiks Tajik, close relatives that understand it. It is written in a form of the Arabic alphabet, from right to left, and everyday writing leaves out most vowels, so the "sounds like" spellings matter more than usual. They put the stressed syllable in capitals, usually the last; aa is a long, deep a, as in "father", ee is a long i and oo a long u.

Spoken and written

Everyday Persian shortens the written forms: چطور است (che-TOR ast), how is it, is said che-TO-re. These phrases use the spoken forms people really use, which are fine in any polite conversation.

No he or she

Persian has no grammatical gender: no he or she, and every phrase is the same whether a man or a woman says it. The verb comes at the end of the sentence.

Being polite: taarof

Use شما (sho-MAA), the respectful you, with anyone you don't know. Iranians practice taarof, an elaborate politeness: a host offers more food, a seller says you needn't pay, a driver says the ride is free. Decline gracefully once or twice, and expect to pay. A thumbs up can be rude; nod or smile instead.

Money and digits

Prices are officially in rials but usually spoken in tomans (10 rials), often without the thousands. Persian digits are used everywhere, written left to right: ۰ ۱ ۲ ۳ ۴ ۵ ۶ ۷ ۸ ۹ are 0 to 9. Foreign bank cards don't work in Iran, so bring cash.
