Sinhala

Sinhala is the language of about three quarters of Sri Lanka's 22 million people; Tamil is the other official language, spoken mostly in the north and east and in the tea country. Sinhala has its own rounded script, written left to right, related to India's scripts but found nowhere else. English is widely understood in Colombo, in hotels, and on the tourist trail, so these phrases are for the smile. The "sounds like" spellings put the leaned-on syllable in capitals, but Sinhala stress is light and even: don't punch it. aa, ee, oo, ay, and oh are long vowels; hold them a little.

Written and spoken

Written Sinhala, on signs and in the news, is quite different from the everyday spoken language. These phrases are spoken Sinhala, the way people talk; where a sign says something else, the note gives the written word, such as දුම්රිය (DUM-ri-ya) on signs for කෝච්චිය (KOHCH-chi-ya), train.

Being polite

Spoken Sinhala tends to leave out "you" and is polite through the verb: ... දෙන්න (DEN-na), please give, and ... පුළුවන්ද? (PU-lu-wan-da), can you...? Greet with ආයුබෝවන් and your palms together. Give and take things with your right hand. At temples, dress modestly, take your shoes and hat off, and never pose with your back to a Buddha statue.

English words

Sinhala borrows English freely: ticket, booking, bus, station, and toilet are all understood. Adding එක (e-ka), one, after an English word makes it "the": බස් එක (BAS e-ka), the bus.
