Hindi

Hindi is spoken by over 600 million people across northern and central India, and understood far beyond. It is written in Devanagari, the script with a line along the top, from left to right. Spoken Hindi and Pakistan's Urdu are almost the same language, so these phrases work there too, though Urdu is written in a different script. The "sounds like" spellings put the stressed syllable in capitals; aa, ee and oo are long vowels.

Man or woman

Some verbs change with the speaker's gender. A man says मैं यह ले लूँगा (LOON-gaa), I'll take it, and a woman says ले लूँगी (LOON-gee). Where a phrase differs, the woman's form is shown under it as "A woman says", and the female voice says that form. Many useful phrases avoid the issue: मुझे ... चाहिए (MU-jhe ... CHAA-hi-ye), literally "to me ... is needed", is the same for everyone.

Being polite

Use आप (aap), the respectful you, with anyone you don't know, and add जी (jee) to names and answers: जी हाँ, yes. Polite requests end in -iye: दीजिए (DEE-ji-ye), please give. Greet with नमस्ते and your palms together. Give and take things with your right hand, and take your shoes off before entering a temple or a home.

Lakhs and crores

India counts large numbers differently: a lakh is 100,000, written 1,00,000, and a crore is ten million, 1,00,00,000. Prices are in rupees, ₹.

English everywhere

Hindi borrows English freely, and Indians mix the two all the time: ticket, station, booking, toilet and doctor are all understood. Don't worry about being "pure".
