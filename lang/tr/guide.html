Spelled as it sounds

Turkish uses the Latin alphabet with a few extra letters, and every letter is always said the same way, so once you know them you can read anything aloud. There are no tones and no genders. Stress usually falls on the last syllable, with exceptions such as place names and some common words; the capitals in the "sounds like" spellings mark it.

Endings and word order

Turkish builds words by adding endings, so one word can be a whole sentence: anlamıyorum is I don't understand. The verb comes last, and "to", "from" and "in" are endings too: Ankara'ya is to Ankara, which is why the place comes first in the travel phrases. Endings change their vowel to match the word (vowel harmony): Ankara'ya but İzmir'e.

Being polite

The phrases use siz, the polite you; sen is for friends. Add bey after a man's first name and hanım after a woman's: Ahmet Bey, Ayşe Hanım. Lütfen is please. Shops and restaurants often offer tea, and accepting is friendly.

Numbers and money

Numbers are regular: on bir is 11 (ten one), yirmi beş 25. Prices are in Turkish lira (₺, TL), with a dot between the thousands: 1.500 TL. Bargaining is normal in bazaars but not in shops with price tags.
