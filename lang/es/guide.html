Which Spanish

This is Latin American (Mexican) Spanish, which is understood everywhere. Spain differs in a few words and in two sounds, listed at the bottom. The "sounds like" spellings put the stressed syllable in capitals.

The five vowels

Spanish has only five vowels, and they are always pure and short, never gliding like English ones.

Stress and accents

Words ending in a vowel, n or s are stressed on the second-to-last syllable, and all other words on the last. An accent mark marks an exception and shows you where to stress: teléfono, café, estación. It also tells some words apart: sí (yes) and si (if), té (tea) and te (you).

Questions and exclamations

Spanish opens a question with ¿ and an exclamation with ¡, as well as closing them. The opening mark tells you to raise your voice from the start of the sentence.

You, polite and casual

The phrases here use usted, the polite you, which is right for waiters, shop staff and strangers. Friends and young people use tú. Usted takes the same verb ending as he or she: ¿Habla inglés? is polite, and ¿Hablas inglés? is casual.

Masculine and feminine

Nouns are masculine or feminine, and adjectives follow: un café, una mesa. A speaker changes an ending to match their own gender: soy alérgico (a man) and soy alérgica (a woman).
